Official Incident Report

Staff Training Failure

An investigation into delayed instructions, poor treat placement and a fundamentally unrealistic understanding of response times.

Incident Summary

During what was intended to be a routine training exercise, Dad placed a treat directly in front of CEO Enzo and attempted to issue the command "leave." Unfortunately, the CEO had already assessed the situation, identified an unattended edible asset and taken appropriate action. The treat was consumed. Dad subsequently issued the instruction: "Okay, eat it." The instruction was approximately one treat too late.

Timeline of Events

  • 00:00 — Training Exercise Commences Dad initiated what appeared to be a standard treat-based compliance exercise. CEO remained cooperative.
  • 00:03 — Treat Placement A treat was placed directly in front of the CEO. Management immediately identified the item as edible, unattended and within operational reach.
  • 00:04 — Executive Decision The treat was consumed. No hesitation was recorded.
  • 00:05 — Delayed Instruction Dad attempted to regain control of the exercise. The command "leave" was determined to have arrived after the relevant decision-making window had closed.
  • 00:06 — Retrospective Authorisation Dad issued the instruction: "Okay, eat it." Management confirms that this instruction was successfully completed approximately two seconds earlier.

Evidence Collected

  • One clearly visible treat.
  • One poorly timed instruction.
  • One extremely fast Cockapoo.
  • Zero remaining treats at the scene.
  • Video evidence confirming the sequence of events.
  • Audible retrospective authorisation from Dad.
  • No evidence that the CEO regretted his decision.

Investigation Findings

Following a full review of the available evidence, investigators determined that the incident was not caused by disobedience. The primary cause was identified as staff response-time failure.

Dad placed a high-value edible item within immediate reach of the CEO and then relied upon verbal instructions being delivered before normal treat acquisition procedures could commence. This strategy was found to be operationally unsound.

Of particular concern was Dad's subsequent instruction to "eat it" after the treat had already been consumed. Investigators have classified this as Retrospective Command Syndrome. Further staff training is recommended.

Corrective Action Required

  • Commands should ideally be issued before the CEO acts.
  • Staff should improve treat-placement risk assessments.
  • Reaction times must account for Cockapoo operating speeds.
  • Instructions should not describe events that have already occurred.
  • Additional treats should be made available for remedial training.

Official Case Outcome

🟢 CEO Cleared of Wrongdoing

All allegations of non-compliance have been dismissed. The investigation determined that the CEO acted within reasonable treat-acquisition parameters and that responsibility for the incident rests entirely with the Training Department. Dad has been reminded that saying "okay, eat it" after the CEO has already eaten it does not constitute successful dog training.
